# Spectral obstruction and limit gates

The derived-descent research packet sharpens two distinct software obligations: atom control under limits and operator-level spectral admissibility. They must not be merged into one generic "regularity" score.

## Shifted-ladder counterexample

The preserved shifted bilateral ladder is a mechanical witness that trace-class relative-resolvent control can coexist with a cutoff-defined infinite off-origin atomic trace comb. Therefore resolvent convergence alone does not certify:

- arithmetic support;
- regularization independence;
- spectral provenance;
- finite atomic complexity.

The imported report contains a finite exact coefficient check and high-precision numerical tables. Its original generator was not supplied, so this repository checks the report's digest and selected internal invariants but does not claim independent regeneration.

## Two separate atomic obligations

`atomic_rigidity` asks whether smooth finite-stage data can concentrate into new off-origin atoms. The existing `atomic` route checks only the internal consistency of a finite declared modulus record; an external proof must establish tail uniformity and compact exhaustion.

`ac_counterterm_closure` asks whether an allowed absolutely continuous counterterm class remains absolutely continuous in the chosen limit topology. Interval nonconcentration alone does not exclude singular-continuous limits. A sufficient future contract must state an appropriate uniform-integrability or local `L^p`, `p > 1`, obligation and the topology in which closure is claimed.

These gates are complementary, not interchangeable.

## Bounded-jet orthogonal-prime obstruction

The later constructive note proves a no-go only under its frozen hypotheses:

1. mutually orthogonal prime blocks;
2. the exact declared prime trace transform;
3. uniformly bounded origin-counterterm jet order;
4. trace-class convergence on a domain meeting `0 < Im(z) <= 1/2`;
5. the stated noncancellation or absolute-continuous remainder control when that channel is present.

Under those conditions, prime-block trace-norm additivity and Euler's divergence force an obstruction. The stronger relative-resolvent corollary uses the resolvent power identity.

The theorem does not rule out:

- nonorthogonal interacting gluing;
- a construction initially confined to `Im(z) > 1/2`;
- separately certified analytic or renormalized continuation;
- unbounded-order origin counterterms;
- weaker operator topologies;
- explicitly controlled cancellation outside the frozen hypotheses.

It does not construct the desired operator or imply the Riemann hypothesis.
